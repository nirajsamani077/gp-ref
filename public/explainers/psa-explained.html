PSA Explained

The PSA test

PSA works like a smoke alarm for the prostate. It is useful, but it goes off for burnt toast as well as for fires, and occasionally stays quiet when there is a fire.

Key point

PSA in ng/mL (µg/L). Age-specific thresholds for urgent referral follow NICE NG12 (40 to 49 above 2.5, 50 to 59 above 3.5, 60 to 69 above 4.5, 70 to 79 above 6.5; clinical judgement under 40 and 80 or over). Avoid ejaculation and vigorous exercise for 48 hours before testing, and wait 6 weeks after a urine infection. About 3 in 4 men with a raised PSA do not have cancer, and about 1 in 7 men with prostate cancer have a normal PSA. The UK National Screening Committee recommended in 2026 targeted screening every 2 years for men aged 45 to 61 with a BRCA2 variant, accepted by the UK Government with an NHS England programme planned from 2027; general population screening is not recommended.
